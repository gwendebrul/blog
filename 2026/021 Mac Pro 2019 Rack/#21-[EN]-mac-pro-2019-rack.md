# Purchase of Mac Pro 2019 Rack

![Mac Pro 2019 Rack](images/mac-pro-2019-rack.jpg "Mac Pro 2019 Rack")

## Current server situation

![Current rack situation](images/current-rack.jpg "Current rack situation")

At the moment, I have 2 **servers**, an **HP Proliant DL360 G5** and an **Apple XServe 2008**, running. These are used for storing and **streaming** **TV Shows** and **Movies**.

Both **servers** are only turned on when I want to watch an episode of a **TV Show** or **Movie**, so the consumption is manageable. However, they each consume 20 watts at standby. That is 350.4 kWh per year just for being plugged in.

Additionally, I have a **mac mini 2014** that serves as a **File server**. This contains files such as certain versions of programs or **documentation** for **programming**, but also all my **eBooks** and some other files, totaling 350GB.

This **mac mini 2014** starts up at 8:00 AM and shuts down at 10:00 PM. During those times, it consumes 25 watts, which is 127.75 kWh per year.

Together, that is 478.15 kWh per year. I don't necessarily want to bring this down, but matching that with just 1 **server** would already be good.

Both the **servers** and the **mac mini** are showing their age, especially the **servers**. So, I want something newer now.

## Mac Pro 2019 Rack

I was browsing the [Refurbed](https://www.refurbed.be) site as I often do by chance and saw a **Mac Pro 2019 Rack** version listed for €1,699. This immediately appealed to me—a **powerful**, **expandable** computer with bonus points for its good **looks**.

So, I started doing some research. I spent a week hesitating over whether or not to buy it, then after a week, I finally made the decision and ordered it.

This version had:
- Xeon W3235 CPU
- 32GB RAM
- 256GB SSD
- Radeon 580x

The **SSD** is a bit small, but I have a solution for that problem.

## Usage of Mac Pro 2019 Rack

Now I want to use this for 4 things. First of all, for the storage of **TV Shows** and **Movies**. This will be done through a **Sonnet J3i** module; it can hold 2x **3.5"** **hard drives** and 1x **2.5"** **hard drive**.

A second reason for its existence is for installing **VMs**. I have a few **VMs** running Linux that I use for experimentation, and I would like to move those over.

A third reason is **local AI**; I am also experimenting with **local AI**, currently still on my **MacBook Pro M1 Pro**, but I want a separate **computer** for those experiments. This way, I won't be tied to **memory requirements** for my next laptop, and I can buy a **MacBook Air** or even a **MacBook Neo** instead of a high-spec **MacBook Pro**.

And a final reason is that I want to use the **Mac Pro 2019 Rack** as a **File server**. For that, I have ordered an **NVMe SSD** and a **PCIe card**.

## Local AI

Here, the more **memory bandwidth**, the better. I was curious about the number of **tokens per second** for a model like **Gemma4 12b Q4_K_M**, and it is quite manageable. I am getting approximately **6 - 8 tokens/second**, which in my opinion is fast enough to get started. That is slightly faster than the speed at which you can read.

But the great thing about the **Mac Pro 2019 Rack** is that it is expandable with 2 specific **GPUs**, namely the **Radeon Vega II with 32GB VRAM** or the **Radeon Pro W5700X with 16GB VRAM**, and you can install 2 **GPUs** for a total of **32 or 64GB of VRAM**.

But that is for the future.

## Conclusion

In my opinion, I got a good deal with this **Mac Pro 2019 Rack**, and if the **macOS** version becomes too old, I can simply continue with **Linux**.

It was a significant purchase, but that is what I save for—to be able to buy things like this.
